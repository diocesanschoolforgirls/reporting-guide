Why reports matter

Back to top

A report is how a family hears, in writing, how their daughter is getting on and what she can do next. It is worth getting right. Many people will help you check your comments, but one rule sits above the rest: you are responsible for each of your comments — their accuracy, appropriateness, professional tone and quality.

This page gathers the dates, the three-stage process, who does what, comment lengths, the length checker, the Dio Dictionary and the dashboards in one place.

03

The three stages

Back to top

The process is the same for subject teachers, tutors and deans. The exception is subjects that are permitted to use proofread comment banks already in Synergetic at mid-year and end of year. If you are unsure, check with your HOF or line manager.

Stage 1: Write

  1. Write your comments in a Google doc (recommended, or similar). Do not type straight into Synergetic. Checking for errors is much harder and slower there, and that is exactly what this process avoids.
  2. Keep an up-to-date class list handy, plus a printout of students with a Specialist Report (SpR).
  3. Use a question mark (?) wherever the student’s name goes. Synergetic turns it into her name when you paste your comments in, and the AI checker recognises it too.
  4. Install Grammarly in your browser (highly recommended) and set it to UK English.
  5. Optional: if report writing is a struggle, the Ultimate Report Writer (an AI tool) can help you draft.

Whether you write every word or use AI support, the comment must be personalised for each student and for each subject, and it must stay within the limits (about 580 characters for subjects, and no more than 640, or 900 for IB subject comments).

Stage 2: Check

  1. Copy your class set from the Google doc into the Ultimate Report Checker. Do one class set at a time. It saves your buddy time.
  2. Use the feedback to correct your comments in your Google doc.
  3. Email your report buddy a link to your Google doc and tell them the comments have been checked with the AI tool.
  4. Your buddy confirms in writing, to you and to the relevant manager (HOF or Dean), that the reports are appropriate and look good.

Stage 3: Finish

  1. Then, and only then, copy and paste your comments into Synergetic (Synweb).
  2. Email your HOF to confirm completion. The HOF will do some spot checks on the accuracy of comments and grade entries.
  3. Done.

How long should a comment be?

Back to top

Lengths are a guide, with a hard technical limit behind them. Synergetic’s usual maximum is 640 characters (IB subject, TOK, CAS and Extended Essay comments can run to 900: see below). Aim for about 580 for subject comments and steer clear of 640. Check your length once names go in (the question mark becomes a name).

IB comments: the 900-character limit

Two sources speak to this, and both are given here.

  • The Staff Handbook says IBDP subject comments have a maximum of 900 characters at mid-year.
  • The Business Information Management (BIM) dashboard checks for over 900 characters for IB subject comments and for IB TOK, CAS and Extended Essay comments. Tutor, dean, Scholarship, boarding and learning support comments are checked against 640 for every student.

In short: IB subject, TOK, CAS and Extended Essay comments can run to 900. Everything else stays within 640. If you are unsure which limit applies to a particular comment, ask your HOF.

The BIM dashboard lists any comment of fewer than 10 words so someone can confirm it is finished. It still counts as written.

What a good comment does, and does not do

Back to top

Write in your own voice. Show that you know the student. The Dio Dictionary conventions help parents read comments from many different authors in the same way.

A good comment

  • Gives honest feedback to parents on academic progress.
  • Is couched in positive language.
  • Suggests strategies for the student to focus on.
  • Gives a target, or feed forward, on what the student can focus on next (looking ahead).

A good comment does not

  • Criticise the student.
  • Use unacceptable language or slang.
  • Provide information that cannot be supported.
  • Simply repeat in words what the grades already show.
  • Concentrate on the child’s personality.

What the BIM dashboard will catch

Back to top

The BIM dashboard reads Senior (Years 12–13) end of year comments from Synergetic every 15 minutes and lists the ones that need correcting. Get these right before you paste in, and your comments will not show up on the list.

Names

  • A name left as “?”
  • When the student’s own name is missing: another student’s name at the start (usually from copying), a misspelt version of her name, her legal first name instead of her preferred name, or a misspelt course or provider name, such as Pamoja (the IB online course provider).

Spaces

  • Hidden (non-breaking) spaces.
  • Two spaces in a row.
  • Comments that are only spaces.
  • Spaces at the start or end are removed automatically (Tidy-up only).

Length

  • Over 640 characters.
  • Over 900 characters for IB subject comments and IB TOK, CAS and Extended Essay comments.
  • Tutor, dean, Scholarship, boarding and learning support comments: 640 for every student.

Very short

  • Fewer than 10 words is listed so someone can check it is finished (it still counts as written).

Handbook conventions

  • NZ spelling.
  • Capitals for IB terms and year levels.
  • Abbreviations written in full.
  • No contractions.
  • Handbook forms such as “examination” and “class work”.

What it will not catch

Every slip. Please still read your comments through. Grades are not checked.

Comments counted as “Teaching” are subject, Scholarship, TOK, CAS, Extended Essay, Boarding and Learning Support, plus Tutor and Dean.

The Dio Dictionary

Back to top

Although a personal writing style is encouraged, these conventions apply so parents can more easily interpret comments written by many authors. They come from the Staff Handbook 2026 (sections 8.0 and 8.1).

No entries match your search. Try a shorter word.

Misuse of words

  • uninterested means a student does not pay attention in class. Do not use “disinterested” (which means impartial).
  • fewer is for countable things (for example “fewer periods doing...”). Less is for uncountable things (for example “less time on...”).

Capitals

  • All subjects are capitalised: French, Biology, Food.
  • Standard numbers: Standard 1.4 (capitalise “Standard” when used as a title with a number).
  • If you use a unit or topic title, capitalise it and follow it with the word “unit” or “topic” (for example “the Broken Bonds unit”).
  • Creative writing is a skill, not a title, so lower case: “Her creative writing needs to...”
  • “tutor group” is not capitalised.
  • “tutor group captain” is not capitalised.

Commas

  • Modern usage has fewer commas. Read it aloud; if it makes sense, let it go.
  • “and” is a conjunction and usually does not need a comma before it.
  • “However” at the start of a sentence is followed by a comma.
  • “however” in the middle of a sentence is preceded and followed by a comma.

Apostrophes

  • Possession: “Sarah has completed an excellent term’s work” (the work belongs to the term, so the apostrophe goes before the s).
  • Plural possession: “Sarah has completed two terms’ work” (the work belongs to two terms, so the apostrophe goes after the s).
  • Plural, no possession: “Sarah has worked very hard over the last two terms” (no apostrophe).
  • No contractions in reports: write “do not”, not “don’t”; “it is”, not “it’s”; “is not”, not “isn’t”.
  • “its” (possessive pronoun) has no apostrophe; “it is” is two words.
  • “hers” (possessive pronoun) has no apostrophe.

Hyphens

  • Modern usage is relaxed about hyphens. Use them when you think it appropriate.

IB Diploma terms

  • Paper 1, Paper 2, Paper 3, Section A, Section B
  • Diploma (not “IB Diploma”, not “Diploma Programme”)
  • CAS
  • “individual knowledge” and “ways of knowing” (lower case)
  • Standard Level (capitalised)
  • Higher Level (capitalised)
  • Extended Essay (capitalised, not “EE”)
  • Option A (capitalised)
  • Internal Assessment (capitalised, not “IA”)
  • Written Assignment (capitalised, not “WA”)
  • Interactive Oral Commentary (capitalised, not “IOC”)
  • Reflective Statement (capitalised, not “RS”)
  • IB learner profile (lower case)

MYP terms

  • Identify formative and summative assessment where needed.
  • Criterion (singular), Criteria (plural).
  • Approaches to Learning (ATL): use the full phrase the first time, then the acronym.
  • Science (the subject name, capitalised) versus science knowledge (lower case when not the subject).
  • Service as Action (SA): use the full phrase the first time, then the acronym.
  • Communication: capitalised when referring to the ATL skill (for example “the Communication skill we were working on”); lower case for general use (for example “the way she communicates with teachers”).
  • Elaborated: capitalised when used as a formal descriptor.
  • IB learner profile (lower case).

A to Z of conventions

  • Achieved: capitalised when referring to the grade itself (for example “She was awarded an Achieved”).
  • achieved: lower case when used as a verb (for example “She has achieved very well in...”).
  • class work: two words.
  • classroom: one word.
  • co-curricular
  • computer program: but “programme” at all other times.
  • cooperative
  • Diocesan: use the full name. “Dio” is not acceptable in reports. Do not use “the School”.
  • DioConnect
  • disinterested: see “uninterested”.
  • do not: no contractions in reports.
  • Education Perfect
  • end of year: lower case, no hyphens.
  • essay writing
  • examination: not “exam”.
  • external
  • extra-curricular
  • focused: one “s”.
  • hard work: two words.
  • hers: no apostrophe (possessive pronoun).
  • Higher Level
  • homework: one word.
  • However: followed by a comma at the start of a sentence.
  • however: preceded and followed by a comma in the middle of a sentence.
  • internal
  • -ise, not -ize: organise, realise (not organize, realize).
  • is not: no contractions.
  • its: no apostrophe (possessive pronoun).
  • Language Perfect
  • led: past tense of “lead” (for example “She led the way...”). Do not write “lead” for the past tense.
  • Level 2: capitalised as a specific title.
  • open-minded
  • organisation / organise: no “z”.
  • practice: the noun (for example “In the practice test...”, “Going to hockey practice...”, “The practice examination was...”).
  • practise: the verb (for example “She needs to practise her...”).
  • proactive
  • programme: except “computer program”.
  • proofread
  • realise: no “z”.
  • school: not capitalised. See “Diocesan”.
  • Standard: not capitalised unless part of a specific title (for example Standard 1.4).
  • Standard Level
  • term four: spell out the number.
  • thoroughly
  • uninterested: means does not pay attention. Not “disinterested”.
  • well-behaved
  • well-mannered
  • William Pike Challenge
  • workload: one word.
  • Year 7: capitalised with a numeral.

Te reo Māori in reports

Back to top

Te reo Māori follows correct linguistic protocols. Short vowel sounds use unaltered lettering (for example, mahi). Long vowel sounds require a macron (for example, mātauranga).

Synergetic does not support macrons. Where a macron would normally appear, use a double vowel instead in report comments entered into Synergetic.

Some words have double vowels that are not macron substitutes (for example, whakaaro). These stay as they are in every setting. If you are unsure, check te Aka Māori Dictionary or ask a colleague with te reo expertise.

Outside Synergetic (in documents, emails and other communications), always use macrons.
